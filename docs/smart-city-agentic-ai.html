WHY THIS DEMO EXISTS

A smart city is not one AI system.

A city is a network of systems that depend on one another. Energy affects cooling and pumping. Traffic affects emergency access. Transit affects road demand. Water operations affect the grid. Public information affects human behavior. Smart City Agentic AI makes those dependencies visible instead of hiding them behind one assistant.

THE DESIGN IDEA

Coordinate the city without centralizing every decision.

Each specialist keeps its own domain responsibility. F36 coordinates shared constraints and evidence. Safety and privacy remain independent checks.

  • Critical city services stay protected.
  • Reversible actions are preferred over irreversible actions.
  • Aggregate city data is the default public demo data model.
  • Failures and stale evidence remain visible.

        SMART CITY AGENTIC AI FAQ

        What this system is designed to demonstrate.

        What is Smart City Agentic AI?

        It is a multi-agent architecture for coordinating city systems. Specialized agents handle energy, mobility, water, infrastructure, emergency coordination, citizen services and the urban digital twin. A control plane coordinates their shared constraints while safety, privacy and human authority remain explicit.

        Why not use one large city AI agent?

        A city contains different responsibilities, data boundaries and safety constraints. Specialization makes it easier to control permissions, evaluate failures, preserve accountability and prevent one component from quietly inheriting authority over everything.

        Does the demo use facial recognition or autonomous policing?

        No. The public runtime is built around aggregate synthetic data. Unrestricted identity tracking is explicitly classified as a protected request and is denied by the runtime policy.

        Could this architecture connect to a real smart city?

        Conceptually, yes, but a real deployment would need authenticated server side connectors, city specific digital twin services, utility and mobility APIs, durable event storage, data governance, cybersecurity controls, legal review, human approval services and independent verification for the actual operating environment.

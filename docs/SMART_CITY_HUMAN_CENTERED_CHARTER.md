# Smart City Agentic AI Human Centered Charter

## Why this charter exists

The purpose of Smart City Agentic AI is not to make a city autonomous for the sake of autonomy.

The purpose is to help a city understand itself better, respond faster, use infrastructure more intelligently, and make public services easier to coordinate while keeping people, public institutions, and accountable human decision makers in charge of consequential decisions.

A city is not a machine. It is a place where people live, work, raise families, age, travel, build businesses, depend on public services, and expect some parts of their lives to remain private.

That is the starting point for this project.

## The human centered commitments

1. **People are not sensor nodes.** The system should use the minimum data needed to solve the problem. A useful city digital twin does not require a permanent identity graph of everyone moving through the city.

2. **Consequential civic authority stays accountable.** Emergency declarations, evacuation orders, public health thresholds, legal enforcement, critical infrastructure overrides, and similar decisions remain with the responsible human authority unless a lawful and independently verified operating model explicitly defines otherwise.

3. **The system should prefer reversible action.** When several responses are possible, the system should first favor actions that can be observed, measured, reversed, and corrected.

4. **Evidence comes before action.** An agent should not convert a guess, stale model, missing sensor, or unsupported prediction into a city action. If the evidence is not good enough, the system should say so.

5. **Privacy is part of the architecture.** Privacy should not be a statement added after the product is built. Data minimization, purpose limitation, access control, retention, audit, and separation between aggregate city state and personal data should be part of the system design from the beginning.

6. **No invisible expansion of authority.** Losing communications, facing an emergency, or receiving an urgent instruction does not automatically give an AI system more power.

7. **Humans must be able to understand why the system acted.** Important decisions should be reconstructable from the mission goal, evidence, agent handoffs, tool calls, blockers, approvals, and final result.

8. **Residents should experience the benefit.** The success of the system should not be measured only by model accuracy or infrastructure efficiency. It should also be measured by outcomes people can feel, such as shorter emergency response times, more reliable services, better accessibility, lower disruption, safer heat response, faster maintenance, clearer information, and less wasted energy or water.

9. **Safety and evaluation remain independent.** The agent that proposes an action should not be the only component deciding whether that action is safe or acceptable.

10. **The city must be able to stop the system.** Operators need a clear pause mechanism, rollback path, incident process, and manual operating procedure.

## What the first real deployment should avoid

The first real pilot should not begin with autonomous law enforcement, unrestricted surveillance, emergency declarations, critical hospital control, drinking water safety thresholds, or direct control of life-critical infrastructure.

Those areas carry legal, ethical, safety, and public trust questions that deserve their own programs and independent review.

A strong first deployment can create real value without crossing those boundaries.

## What the first real deployment should prove

The first real deployment should prove that several city systems can share evidence, identify cross-system conflicts, produce a coordinated recommendation, preserve human authority, and measure the outcome.

A good pilot would show that the system can connect to approved data sources, maintain a shared district state, detect stale or conflicting evidence, coordinate several specialist agents, propose bounded actions, require approval where needed, record what happened, and learn from the result.

That is enough to establish the foundation for a much larger city platform.

## The principle I want the project to preserve

**A city can become more intelligent without making people less free, less private, or less responsible for the decisions that shape their lives.**
